# What we know, and how we know it

Jury question 7 is "have you tested this with a real family?" We have not, and
we are not going to before the 27th: this was built from outside India, on a
hackathon timeline, with no access to Indian families or bank branches. Saying
that plainly is better than implying a field study nobody ran.

What we can do, and did, is show that the problem is documented by people with
far more authority than a founder's anecdote. That is what this file is for.
It is the evidence base behind the product, so that "we did not test with users"
is followed by "and here is what we built it from instead."

## The strongest piece of evidence: the Supreme Court is hearing this

The Supreme Court is hearing a plea by the journalist Sucheta Dalal seeking a
mechanism to inform **legal heirs of deceased depositors** about dormant
unclaimed deposits. Counsel argued that deposits also sit in post offices and
insurance, that nothing has been done to integrate them, and that a legal heir
should be able to find everything in one search.

That is the Virasat problem statement, argued in the Supreme Court of India, by
somebody else. It is better validation than three phone calls, because it is on
the record and a judge can check it.

Source r19 on the Sources page.

## Demand is proven, not assumed

Gujarat's claim camps under Aapki Poonji Aapka Adhikar returned **₹104 crore
across 26,874 claims**, an average of ₹38,700 per family.

This matters more than it looks. It is not a survey of whether families *would*
come forward. It is 26,874 families who *did* come forward, in one state, when
somebody helped them in person. The demand is established; what is missing is
the software the volunteer sits in front of. That is the gap we build into.

## The friction is documented, in the institutions' own terms

- **Searching is not claiming.** Every government portal, including the new
  Common Landing Portal, finds the asset and then hands the family back to the
  institution. No portal tells a family which route applies, which documents to
  gather, or fills a form.
- **The nominee fork is where families stall.** With a nominee, a bank must not
  insist on a succession certificate. Without one, heirs may need a succession
  certificate from a civil court, typically **three to six months**.
- **Coverage gaps remain.** Post office deposits, provident funds and insurance
  are not all traceable in one place.
- **Banks do not prompt.** A published account describes a pensioner's son told
  at the branch that his father had never nominated anyone, and that prompting a
  nomination was not the bank's job. Source r20.

## What the government solved in May 2026, and what it did not

On 29 May 2026 the Department of Financial Services launched the **Common
Landing Portal for Unclaimed Financial Assets** with the PSB Alliance: one
search across bank deposits, insurance, shares, dividends and mutual funds.

Concede this immediately if asked. It is a genuine improvement and it removes
one of the things we used to claim as a difference. It is also **search only**:
no document reading, no claim route, no forms, no tracking. The capability
table on the judges page says so row by row, and the first row now gives the
government the credit.

## How to answer question 7 on the 27th

Say, in this order:

1. No. Not with a real family, and it is the most important thing missing.
2. Here is why: built from outside India in a hackathon window, with no access
   to families or branches. Do not dress it up.
3. Here is what it was built from instead: documented procedures from SBI, LIC,
   EPFO, IEPF and India Post, and public sources, every one cited on the Sources
   page with a link.
4. Here is who else says the problem is real: the Supreme Court is hearing a
   plea about legal heirs of deceased depositors being unable to find their
   money, and Gujarat's camps returned ₹104 crore to 26,874 families.
5. Here is the first thing a pilot measures: rupees reaching families per week,
   in one district, with service centre operators. Not sign ups.

Then stop talking.

## What this file is not

It is not a claim that desk research equals user testing. It does not. A judge
who says "that is still not validation" is right, and the answer is "agreed,
and it is the first thing the pilot buys." Do not argue that point. Conceding
it costs nothing and arguing it costs the room.
